# Focuswilds Implementation Plan

Approved requirements, not a checklist of shipped features. README and this plan remain English. The application supports English and Thai, defaults to English, and is restricted to the owner account.

## Approved cat-care game direction (2026-10-18; not implemented)

The player collects and cares for cats of different breeds. Use "player", not trainer; cats are individual residents, not evolving monsters or expendable workers. Focus earns money, player experience and items that can be spent on the cats and their shared living space. This replaces survivor production, expedition combat, equipment wear, carrying limits and permanent death as the active product plan. The mobile PWA, owner-only access and Aseprite requirements remain in force.

The currently implemented application is still the survivor shelter with a landscape runtime, native inputs and its existing save schema. The cat economy, shop, affection, mood, effects, room expansion and new focus modes are planned only. See DEVELOPMENT.md and SHELTER_SCHEMA.md for the existing implementation and technical migration boundaries. Do not rewrite old saved data or describe the replacement as shipped.

### Core loop and focus rewards

1. Choose a task or focus title, select count-up or countdown, and focus. Retain pause/resume, partial-session recording and recovery; add a separate rest phase. Bound reward-eligible elapsed time and define rest calculation/caps before shipping the new count-up mode. Rest, unattended background rendering and merely reopening the app do not generate focus rewards.
2. Settle verified focus duration into integer money, player XP and an item outcome. Player levels unlock content/capacity; cats gain affection through care rather than focus XP or evolution. No real-money payments, paid pulls or premium currency.
3. Buy or use food, treats, toys and other care items, interact with cats, and improve the room. Show what a care action consumes and its expected affection/mood effect before confirmation.
4. Higher affection increases the chance of beneficial cat effects. Skipping care across reward-eligible focus rounds can worsen mood and create a chance of an adverse effect. Apply a clear, bounded effect to a later focus settlement, then use the earned resources for further care and progress.

Both completed and partial focus must have a documented reward policy. Use actual eligible duration, not the selected target; splitting/retrying a session or logging the same time through a task must not duplicate base rewards. Define a minimum eligible care/reward round and a duration-based effect budget so repeated tiny sessions cannot farm rewards or accelerate mood penalties. Task checkmarks and tags organize work without paying the same focus time twice. Initial scope includes simple to-dos, subtasks, tags and daily focus goals; habits, time-block routines and self-defined real-life rewards are deferred.

### Cats, affection and mood

- Catalog breeds with stable IDs and readable breed-specific art. Keep each adopted cat's identity, breed, affection and mood separate from the shared breed catalog. Initial breed count, duplicate-breed policy, adoption requirements and numeric balance are implementation decisions to document before release, not assumed unlimited collections.
- A starter cat and a usable basic room/care path must allow progress from the first session. Unlock further adoption through player progress and available room capacity. No forced daily portal visit, real-time breeding/genetics system or evolution requirement is included.
- Affection is the lasting relationship built through care and gifts. Mood is the shorter-term care state. Keep both bounded and visible; high affection raises good-effect probability, while poor mood can grant a bad effect. Document how they combine so the UI can explain which outcome is possible.
- Owner-confirmed: missed care advances only through committed eligible focus/play rounds, never wall-clock absence. Closed apps, paused timers, rest, failed writes, duplicate retries, shop browsing and reopening dialogs do not count as missed care. No background decay, daily-login punishment, cat death, escape or loss of adopted cats.
- Record care applied since the previous eligible round and evaluate it once at settlement. Define whether each care type covers one cat or multiple cats; taking an item out of inventory is not care until its validated use succeeds. A failed care action cannot spend an item or improve affection.
- Use appropriate care beyond buying the most expensive gift; define a recoverable basic care option. Negative effects must not create a zero-income trap that prevents the player from caring for cats again. Do not silently add disease, veterinary simulation or passive survival meters.

### Cat effects and balance

- First effect scope is temporary modifiers to newly earned game money, player XP or item chances. Do not modify elapsed focus time, user-entered task completion, existing account balances, saved history or real-device behavior. Never remove already earned XP, levels or cats as a mood penalty.
- Show the source cat, effect, affected reward, duration/remaining uses, probability and stacking limit. Show adverse effects clearly before the affected focus round starts; do not reveal an undisclosed penalty only after work is done. A bad-mood roll at settlement therefore affects a subsequent round, not a retroactive deduction.
- Snapshot eligible effects when a focus round starts; changing care selections/room contents or switching devices mid-round must not reroll them. Resolve random outcomes once from a stable operation/session identity, persist the receipt and reuse it on retry. Starting/canceling sessions must not offer free rerolls.
- Set numeric caps, minimum positive reward, overlap rules and rounding policy together with tests. A higher-affection chance is not a guaranteed good effect every round. Basic focus rewards must remain useful when every cat is unhappy.

### Cat room, inventory and shop

- The home screen is a cat room or sheltered cat area. Decorate with toys, cat houses/beds, food bowls, water bowls and other cat objects. Display the cats living there and the player's current care/progress; the player level and room upgrades can raise adoption capacity.
- Begin with fixed decoration positions and place/remove actions, not a freeform building editor. Give every functional decoration a disclosed effect; distinguish it from cosmetic objects. Placed objects cannot simultaneously be used up or sold, and removal returns them to inventory without creating duplicates. Never discard a cat when capacity or placement changes.
- Use one bounded catalog of item IDs, prices, allowed actions and effects. The shop buys supported items from the player and sells care items/decorations for game money. Show quantity, unit price, total and any non-sellable status before confirmation. No fluctuating market, player trading or real-money purchase flow.
- Validate integer quantities, sufficient funds/stock, item ownership, capacity and placement at the trusted command boundary. Determine prices and reward effects from catalog data, never caller-supplied totals. Shop purchases/sales, item use, adoption and placement are atomic and idempotent. Prevent buy/sell or placement loops from creating money/items; consumables cannot be sold after use.

### Decisions to calibrate during implementation

Document the initial money/XP rates and item pool, cat breeds and adoption costs, level/capacity thresholds, affection gains, mood tiers and missed-care thresholds, effect chances/caps/durations, room catalog and buy/sell prices. Define task/goal rewards, if any, separately from focus settlement. These are not final values or approved claims of measured balance. Test a zero-money start, all cats unhappy, partial sessions, full capacity, simultaneous tabs, duplicate/lost responses, and purchase/use/sale races before expanding content.

## Mobile PWA priority update (2026-10-18; implementation pending)

This latest owner-approved direction supersedes earlier landscape-first and page-zoom-preservation requirements. Prioritize an installed portrait mobile experience on iOS and Android before continuing Aseprite asset integration. Keep the approved world, material detail, palette, depth and Aseprite authoring workflow, with cats replacing the survivor-management subject under the latest game direction above. Dated checkpoints in DEVELOPMENT.md describe earlier implementations, not acceptance of this new behavior. The current runtime still rotates portrait viewports into landscape and the manifest still requests landscape; the install manifest and Settings guidance already exist, but the new layout, keyboard and fixed-scale behavior have not shipped.

- Portrait is the logical layout on every screen. In a landscape viewport, rotate the whole application by 90 degrees into a portrait layout; leave portrait and square viewports upright. Apply the same rule before React starts and to login, loading, recovery, signed-in controls, top-layer dialogs and the in-game keyboard. Do not use a rotation gate or require fullscreen/OS orientation lock. Determine orientation from the underlying viewport, not a keyboard-reduced content region, so editing cannot trigger rotation.
- Design the main screen for one-handed touch use, readable scene framing, reachable primary actions and safe areas around notches/home indicators. Keep the shell within one viewport without document scrolling or horizontal overflow. Let long dialogs and the active editing region scroll internally without visible scrollbars. Preserve timer identity/deadline, unsaved drafts, selection and dialog state across resize, rotation, keyboard changes and background/resume; these events must not fetch game state again.
- The ordinary app scale should stay fixed: prevent app-level pinch/double-tap scaling where the browser permits and avoid focus-triggered zoom. Keep editable control text at least 16px. Browser/OS accessibility overrides can still allow zoom; this PWA must not claim an unconditional zoom lock or native-app equivalence on untested devices. Preserve semantic labels, screen-reader navigation, hardware-keyboard operation and readable controls.
- The owner selected a custom in-game keyboard for all first-party text and numeric entry. Build one shared keyboard/editor, styled with GNU Unifont and the game's palette, with Thai/English text layouts and a numeric layout. Use one active editor at a time. Support caret/selection replacement, spaces, case/symbols, deletion that respects user-perceived characters including Thai marks, and clear Done/Cancel actions. Retain existing validation, length limits and disabled states. Keep draft edits local until confirmation; Cancel restores the original value. Read-only/disabled fields must not open an editor. Selection controls and checkboxes remain game-styled choices, not text-keyboard fields.
- Dock the custom keyboard inside the same rotated app frame or active dialog, reserving its space explicitly. Keep the outer shell stationary; bring only the active editing region into view above the keyboard. Do not push the whole page upward, place the keyboard behind a modal, dismiss its parent dialog accidentally or lose edits on rotation. Keep programmatic editing compatible with physical keyboards, IME composition and assistive technology. Suppress the system soft keyboard where supported, with a tested usable fallback when a browser ignores the hint; never leave a field impossible to edit. External provider authentication and OS permission screens cannot use the in-game keyboard.
- Deliver through the existing PWA shell: retain the scoped manifest/start URL, icons and installation guidance, request portrait orientation, and use standalone display where supported. Manifest orientation alone is not a cross-browser lock. Test both browser and installed modes on real iOS/Android devices, including system navigation and app resume. Keep offline startup outside this change; do not add a service worker or promise offline saving merely to label the app a PWA. Existing save-error and timer recovery behavior remains required.

### Implementation order and acceptance

1. Replace the shared landscape rotation contract in `index.html`, `ScreenLayout`, global/container styles, dialogs and manifest with the portrait contract. Reframe the login camera and signed-in layout for portrait, preserving asset anchors and world scale. Review the portrait composition before producing replacement art; do not simply crop away the survivors or main controls.
2. Add the shared editor/keyboard to the existing first-party entry points: focus title and custom focus minutes, then all new task, tag, cat-care and shop entry points. Audit all other input/select/textarea callers, including future ones, so no editable field is missed. Use draft numeric text so deleting and retyping a value does not reset the timer or commit an invalid intermediate value. Keep existing domain limits and commit through the current state handlers.
3. Verify fixed-scale gestures, focus behavior, custom keyboard placement and dialog focus restoration. Use feature detection for viewport/system-keyboard fallbacks; do not require the limited-availability VirtualKeyboard API or a new framework. Test Thai combining characters, English, symbols, selection/deletion, hardware IME input, confirm/cancel, validation errors, disabled fields and provider sign-in separately.
4. Update the former landscape assertions in screen-layout/PWA tests and add focused keyboard/viewport tests. Run offline tests, lint and build, then browser checks for portrait/landscape/square, short viewports, safe areas, loading/failure, every dialog, system-keyboard fallback and timer continuity. Real-phone acceptance must include iOS Safari plus home-screen mode and Android Chrome plus installed mode. Record actual OS/browser versions, gesture/keyboard behavior, frame pacing and load bytes; browser emulation alone is insufficient.
5. Resume Aseprite production only after the portrait shell and representative editing flow work together. Integrate one cat, then a care interaction and a layered room object, before expanding scenes. No new database reads/writes, paid service or native wrapper is authorized by this planning update. If strict platform-wide zoom/keyboard guarantees prove impossible in PWA, report the measured limitation before proposing a different platform.

## Approved visual direction

The cat-care subject update preserves the existing medieval world after humanity's collapse: a small sheltered place amid old stone, damaged timber, mist and uncertain folklore beyond. Shift the playable center to a safe room or sheltered area for cats. Keep charcoal/graphite materials, ash/bone-white light and restrained ember-red warmth. No unrequested switch to a modern apartment, bright pastel world, industrial machinery or steampunk. The narrative is now about caring for cats; survivor combat and ruined-world threats do not become active danger to the cats.

- Use compact, readable pixel cats with breed-distinct silhouettes in a detailed environment with real/layered depth, elevation, contact shadows and foreground occlusion. A reduced detailed illustration or flat full-scene parallax is not sufficient. Keep lighting consistent between cats, objects and ground; readable controls and mood/effect labels must not rely on color alone.
- Keep the existing ruined-courtyard login as a temporary implemented scene until the new portrait cat composition is reviewed. The old requirement for four seated survivors and their 1.7-unit scale is retired. Plan a portrait cat-area login and home composition using the shared environment language, centered title/text-only sign-in, visible keyboard focus and the existing loading/failure treatment. Do not assume an interior room layout or final cat count has been visually accepted.
- Use a fixed elevated three-quarter camera and coherent world scale. Define cat size, sprite cells, foot anchors, viewing directions, idle/walk/care states and object occlusion before drawing a complete breed set. Adapt camera framing to portrait/rotated landscape without stretching or moving objects independently at breakpoints. Keep all primary actions readable with the keyboard open.
- Draw discrete idle/care poses. Begin walking with six distinct poses at approximately eight changes per second only where real paths require walking. Animate position separately, without sliding feet or stretching as a substitute for poses. Keep the home camera still and motion subtle; existing fire/mist/sparks remain restrained if present in the approved composition.
- Aseprite is the primary authoring tool for cats, room artwork, objects, effects and illustrated UI. Retain React for accessible live text/controls and the existing Three.js scene infrastructure. Export separate transparent objects and sprite sheets; keep geometry/elevation/occluders for spatial support. Do not replace static scenery with a full-scene animation loop or add a renderer/framework without a concrete requirement.
- Preserve current asset dimensions/frame sampling while old assets are still used. New cats need their own measured contract; never relabel a human sprite sheet as cat art. Export PNG plus only metadata consumed by the application, compress tested runtime assets, and keep editable sources with backups outside deployed assets. Runtime JSON import is not currently implemented. See DEVELOPMENT.md for the current WebP export checks and Aseprite setup.
- One lifecycle handles loading, state updates, resize, continuous animation, visibility suspension, graphics failure/recovery and disposal. Keep the timer mounted. Run animation regardless of OS reduced-motion settings; suspend only while hidden or graphics are unavailable. Scene/keyboard animation performs zero database operations.
- Measure transfer bytes, deployed assets, decoded textures, frame-time percentiles, dropped frames, draw calls and memory on desktop and real phones. Keep assets bundled locally on free hosting, reuse atlases and release unused graphics resources. The former 500 kB baseline is not a hard cap. No GIF/video loops, remote asset requests or unused full-resolution originals. Target stable 60 FPS where capable and a measured 30 FPS fallback; do not claim either before testing.
- Validate one cat, a care interaction and one layered room object together before expanding breeds or furnishings. Check silhouette, anchor stability, transparency, lighting, depth, portrait framing and editing overlays in the running app. Concept art alone is not acceptance. Day/night simulation, combat and multiple travel environments are outside the new scope.

## Languages and signed-in Settings

- Support English (`en`) and Thai (`th`) across menus, account messages, cats, breeds, care actions, moods, effects, items, shop entries, tasks and tags, and accessibility labels. Keep translated strings separate from logic and use stable IDs for game data.
- Default to English when an account has no saved language. The logged-out screen uses English; Settings is available only after login as a game-style icon dialog.
- Persist the selected language in the account's Cloud Firestore data, using the existing Firebase Spark project without Billing. Local storage alone does not satisfy account persistence.
- Load the preference with account data and reuse it in memory. Reopening Settings must not read Firestore again. Write only when the saved language changes.
- Apply the saved preference when signing in on another device. Without listeners or polling, an already open device picks up remote changes through an explicit refresh or the next account load.
- Validate supported language codes at application and rules boundaries. Add preference operations to the documented read/write budget when the schema is defined; do not assume they are free.
- Show loading/save failures with retry. Do not report a preference as saved before the write succeeds, and do not silently overwrite an unread preference after a load failure.
- Switching language must preserve the mounted timer, cat-room state, local drafts, and progress. Update document language, dates, and accessible labels consistently.
- Test default English, both translations, persistence, invalid values, failed saves, repeated Settings opens, and switching during an active timer using the offline store.
- README and this implementation plan remain English. Thai is an application language option, not a requirement to translate repository documents.

## Technical structure, persistence and migration

- Keep this a personal application restricted to the owner account in both application checks and data rules. No social rooms, public profiles, party/chat system, marketplace or multiplayer infrastructure. Keep Firebase Spark without Billing and all deployed services free.
- Reuse reliable timer/deadline recovery, transaction receipts, revision checks, validation, summary history, language settings, dialog focus handling and the scene lifecycle. Keep the existing project layers: pure game commands/content, persistence adapter/store, and React UI. Do not rewrite working infrastructure merely to rename the game. Split by concrete domain responsibility when the replacement needs it, without a new framework or speculative plugin system.
- Define a versioned player/cat/room/inventory state and operation contracts before implementing the new economy. A focus settlement must atomically commit duration/history summaries, money, player XP, items, eligible-round mood changes and persisted effect outcomes exactly once. Buy/sell/use/adopt/place operations need the same identity/revision safety. Validate all amounts, ownership, capacities, prices and effect bounds before writing; cap document/collection growth before release.
- Keep existing progress intact. Do not auto-reset, delete survivor state, reinterpret old materials as money, or convert survivor XP into player XP. Present an explicit migration/archive decision before rollout; conversion rules and any destructive cleanup require owner authorization. Preserve historical focus records and pending unsaved sessions. Old schema documentation remains valid for the current app until migration ships.
- No realtime listeners, polling, refetch on focus/visibility or unbounded retries. Load history on demand in pages of at most 25; bound daily summaries to 30 and paginate all-time days. Use account summaries for totals; do not rescan session history. Deduplicate simultaneous reads and reuse transaction results. Keep Asia/Bangkok across devices.
- Document actual read/write costs for the new commands and migration before rollout. The existing budgets do not automatically apply to new cat/shop writes. Import/migration work must be locally validated, bounded, resumable and idempotent without overwriting existing progress. Routine tests remain in-memory with the network-blocking preload, no Emulator or CLI dependency and no production services.
- Offline startup and offline economy writes are not added by the PWA plan. Keep failed focus settlement recoverable without duplicate payouts; a failed purchase must not be displayed as owned. Do not cache provider credentials or make unverified claims about production rules or phone installation.
